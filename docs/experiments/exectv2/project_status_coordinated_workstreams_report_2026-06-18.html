Key Insights From The Process

The process worked because parallelism was applied to independent surfaces and gates, while the riskiest work was deliberately converted into checklists instead of runs.

Ownership metadata is now a core deliverable.

ExECTv2 cannot be judged only by score deltas. The same output can mean different things depending on whether a fact was selected by the model, normalized by a deterministic adapter, or rendered for benchmark compatibility.

The SF bottleneck is clinical detail selection, not citation.

The ledger still shows zero evidence failures. That points attention away from evidence mechanics and toward coverage, state selection, and phrase-to-anchor conventions.

The best next SF change is narrow and testable.

Anchor normalization can recover selected phrases without letting deterministic code choose new seizure states, counts, or temporal anchors. That is exactly the kind of adapter work the project can safely absorb.

CUI gains are useful only when they remain boring.

The four added mappings improved coverage because they were recurrent, one-to-one, and projection-only. The remaining gaps should not be rushed into the lexicon just to make a metric prettier.

Blocked work became more actionable by staying blocked.

The family-routed comparison, Gan holdout-facing work, and full-200 audits are now blocked for concrete missing artifacts rather than vague caution. That is a better state than an unauthorized run.

Parallel threads should converge through artifacts, not memory.

The strongest outputs were tests, preflight modules, ledgers, and runbooks. They give the coordinator something mergeable and reviewable, even when different worktrees touched overlapping status language.
