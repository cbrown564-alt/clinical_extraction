## Answer

RQ7 is partly answered, but not fully closed:

```text
The hidden-family evidence is strong enough to guide architecture, but not
complete enough to make per-family holdout-transfer claims for every component.
Boundary, uncertainty, seizure-free, current-vs-historical, and competing-state
families define the useful LLM role. Rate, denominator, cluster, diary, and
benchmark-convention families still need more controlled component tests.
```

The most durable generalization lesson is negative: validation-prefix success
does not imply hidden-family transfer. A2 sparse operands looked strong through
validation250, then collapsed on later validation rows. Broad typed operations
and graph projection repeated the same failure pattern by duplicating decision
ownership and letting deterministic sidecars overrule useful selected evidence.

The positive transfer signal is selective and gated. `selective_safety_floor_gate_v0`
preserves zero correct-to-wrong changes on validation750 and the frozen local
test450 audit, with gains concentrated in boundary and current-state slices.

## Prefix-Versus-Later Validation Lesson

A2 is the cleanest warning. It scored 232/250 on the validation250 selected
evidence arithmetic layer, but the validation750 split showed a later-500 drop
to 337/500. Sparse-operand adapter regressions rose from 5 selected-evidence
correct to adapter-wrong rows in the first 250 to 32 in the later 500.

This means validation prefixes and fixed hard panels should be treated as
contract and mechanism surfaces. They are not enough to promote a schema unless
the hidden-family transfer table remains stable on broader validation or a
frozen audit.

## Decision

RQ7 is answered as an architecture-guidance synthesis, not as a complete
per-family transfer claim:

- Use hidden-family tags in every future component answer.
- Prefer selective, exact-evidence, no-regression gates over broad replacement.
- Treat A2/typed-operations success on prefixes as non-transferable until the
  later-validation and family tables agree.
- Do not claim holdout-transfer by hidden family unless the protocol exposes
  predeclared slices, changed-row accounting, and inspection limits.

## Remaining Controlled Work

No broad validation F1 run is needed. The missing RQ7 work is a family-indexed
component matrix that joins, for each family:

- RQ1 candidate recall/rescue;
- RQ2 exact evidence and source-id validity;
- RQ3 boundary-field completeness;
- RQ4 projection W->C/C->W;
- RQ5 rendering drift;
- RQ6 selective-action changed-row precision;
- RQ9 abstain/review/monitoring burden;
- RQ10 ambiguity/convention class.

This can start as a no-new-call artifact over saved outputs. Holdout-facing
family claims should wait until the candidate and slice definitions are frozen.
