Parallel holdout comparison

Two locked tests. Two different stories.

Historical two-method holdout view: on each task, Grok with rules is compared with the other methods reported for that locked test at the time. Read the panels separately. They use different scores and answer different clinical questions. Gemini is now the cited model; this page is not the five-cell headline table.

What this does not proveOn both locked tests shown here, the proposed method is highest among the methods in that panel. That is not state of the art, not clinical validation, and not proof that a model plus recorded rules always wins. The golds are evaluation forms, not the task.

Compare methods down each panel. Do not compare a Gan bar with an ExECT bar: the two scores answer different questions.

What the paired view supports

On Gan, the fair comparison is cleaned hybrid versus Grok LLM-only: +0.11 (375 vs 327 of 450). Rules-only is the deterministic floor (329/450). On ExECT, all three methods use clinical fact F1; rules-only (0.7937) is already close to Grok with rules (0.805). These are task-specific outcome descriptions. They do not say which pipeline stages caused the changes.

Do not add or rank the tasks

Gan scores one final frequency label. ExECT scores a set of findings across four families. A higher number in one panel is not evidence that its task is easier or that its system is better.

A sentence this page supports

On Gan’s locked 450-letter test, Grok with rules scored 0.8333 Purist accuracy (375/450), 48 letters above Grok LLM-only. On ExECT’s locked 60-letter test, Grok with rules scored 0.805 clinical-fact F1, above Grok LLM-only 0.7726 and rules-only 0.7937. These are separate comparisons. They are not clinical validation or a claim of state of the art.
